### History and archaeology
- Wikipedia, "Jeroboam II": reign c. 786–746 BC (Albright; Thiele differs); prosperity; 1904
  Megiddo seal "Shema, servant of Jeroboam" (not used in handout); Samaria ostraca dated to his reign.
- Britannica, "Book of Hosea" (search snippet; full page blocked by Cloudflare): Hosea began
  under Jeroboam II, c. 786–746 BC. Second source for the reign dates.
- World History Encyclopedia, Rebecca Denova, "Hosea": ministry from Jeroboam II (787–747) to
  Hoshea (731–722); 722 BC fall of the north. Cross-check on dates.
- Wikipedia, "Hoshea," "Menahem," "Tiglath-Pileser III," "Shalmaneser V," "Sargon II": Pul =
  Tiglath-pileser III (745–727); Menahem's tribute; Hoshea installed by Assyria; three-year siege;
  fall 722/721; Sargon claims ~27,280 resettled (handout says "about 27,000"; other editions of the
  annals read 27,290).
- 2 Kings 14–17 (Gospel Library): 41-year reign; six kings after Jeroboam II, four assassinated
  (15:10, 14, 25, 30); 15:19 Pul; 17:4–6 Hoshea, So of Egypt, three-year siege, deportation.
- Wikipedia, "Black Obelisk of Shalmaneser III": Nimrud, erected 825 BC, found by Layard 1846,
  British Museum; "Yaua of Bit Omri" usually = Jehu (minority view: Jehoram); earliest known
  depiction of a biblical figure. (British Museum's own page refused automated fetch.)
- Wikipedia, "Samaria Ostraca": 102 sherds (63 legible), Reisner 1910, wine and fine oil
  receipts, mid-8th c., Istanbul Archaeology Museums; Jeroboam II attribution "compelling" (Rollston).
- Biblical Archaeology Society (via search: "Yahweh or Baal: Who Was the God of Northern
  Israel?" image pages) — ostraca names mostly honor Yahweh, far fewer honor Baal. Used for the
  "most names honor the Lord, some built on Baal" line (no exact ratio given; deliberately vague).
- Wikipedia, "Kuntillet ʿAjrud": c. 801–770 BC by C-14; inscriptions "YHWH of Samaria";
  Israelite outpost; debate over the drawings. Used with "and his asherah" (standard reading of
  the inscription; whether asherah = goddess or cult object is debated, said so in handout).
- Wikipedia, "Book of Joel": dating proposals (9th c. to post-exilic; Greeks in 3:6); literal
  locusts read apocalyptically vs. allegory.
- 1915 Jerusalem locust plague: Mosaic Magazine and Tablet Magazine reviews, Jerusalem Quarterly
  56–57 (Wichhart), BiblePlaces: John D. Whiting, "Jerusalem's Locust Plague," *National
  Geographic*, Dec. 1915; plague March–October 1915; Whiting quotes Joel heavily.

### Uncertain / flagged in the handout
- Literal vs. symbolic marriage (Hosea 1–3); whether the woman in Hosea 3 is Gomer.
- End of Hosea's ministry (BD: before 736 BC; others: into the 720s).
- "King Jareb" (5:13; 10:6): unidentified; "great king" title is a suggestion, marked as "may be."
- Joel's date: unknown.
- Black Obelisk "Yaua" = Jehu: majority view, labelled "most likely."
- Kuntillet ʿAjrud "asherah": goddess vs. cult object, labelled debated.
- Hosea 6:1–2 "third day" as a type of the Resurrection: labelled an interpretation.
- "Hesed" in 2:19, 4:1, 6:4, 6:6, 10:12, 12:6: from the Hebrew text (Scripture Helps confirms
  2:19); verses 4:1/6:4/10:12/12:6 rely on standard lexical knowledge, not a fetched source.
- Beth-aven = "house of wickedness," Jehoshaphat = "the Lord judges," Achor = "trouble": standard
  name glosses (Achor's link to Achan verified in Joshua 7:24–26).
